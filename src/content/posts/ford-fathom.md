Almost exactly a year ago, [I wrote about Ford's Skunkworks project](/posts/ford-skunkworks-ev-strategy) and called it a bet-the-company move. A separate building in California, stuffed with Tesla and Apple and Rivian people, working on an affordable EV platform while Jim Farley reportedly couldn't even badge in. Today Ford showed its cards. The truck is called the Fathom, and it starts at $28,350.

Before anyone gets too excited about that number, let's do the math Ford's press team hopes you won't. Destination and delivery is $1,595, so the real starting price is $29,945. Add taxes and fees and you're driving off the lot at something like $32,000. But credit where it's due: Ford spent two years saying "under $30,000," and they landed under $30,000 even with destination included. In an industry where the advertised price and the actual price usually live in different zip codes, hitting your stated target is almost shocking behavior.

## About that name

A fathom is six feet, the span of your outstretched arms, the unit for measuring water depth. The internet was betting hard on Ford reviving the Ranchero name, and I'll admit that would have been fun. Instead, two Ford copywriters independently pulled Fathom out of a file of hundreds of candidates. Ford says the naming team kept polling engineers between meetings, and one engineer who happened to match the truck's target customer profile picked the eventual winner every single time. They let him hear the final decision first, which is a nice touch.

The six-foot thing has people speculating about bed length. I have no inside information, but I like the way they're thinking.

## What we actually know

Ford is still holding the full reveal for early 2027, when pre-orders open, with deliveries later that year. But between the announcement and what's leaked out over the past year, the picture is pretty detailed. This is a clean-sheet midsize truck, roughly Maverick-sized, with more interior passenger space than a Toyota RAV4, plus a frunk and a bed. The standard battery is LFP, structural, cell-to-pack, widely estimated around 51 kWh, with cells from Ford's Marshall, Michigan plant. Rear-wheel drive standard, dual-motor AWD later. It's built with a single giant aluminum casting, about 20% fewer parts than a conventional Ford, and a new "assembly tree" process at the Louisville plant instead of the moving line Henry Ford invented. It'll be the first Ford with a native NACS charge port. Every truck ships with the hardware for next-gen Blue Cruise, plus a big touchscreen, digital key, bidirectional power, CarPlay, and Android Auto.

Every one of those engineering choices points the same direction: taking cost out of the vehicle rather than winning a spec sheet. That's the entire thesis of the platform, and it's why this truck matters more than any 800-volt, quad-motor halo product announced this year.

## The Lightning in the room

I owned an F-150 Lightning for years and loved it. I've since sold it and moved to a Model X, and before anyone accuses me of abandoning my own buy-what-you-need sermon: the X earned its spot. It's big and roomy, tows 5,000 pounds, and the cargo floor is flat enough that I can car camp in it at 6'3", which is a fathom plus three inches, apparently. I also caught it while the Luxe package still existed, so free Supercharging and free FSD for the life of the car. You can't buy either of those anymore at any price, and "fuel" never gets cheaper. Ford stopped building the Lightning because the giant battery that makes it a great truck also made it impossible to sell at a profit, and a plug-in hybrid version is coming to carry the name forward. Tom Moloughney, a four-year Lightning owner, [made this point well in his announcement video](https://youtu.be/M4n4C85t5iU): the Fathom exists because the Lightning couldn't. And the price gap is stark. This truck starts at less than half of what the cheapest Lightning ever sold for.

The tradeoff is range, and I want to be honest about it since the fanboys won't be. A 51 kWh pack in a truck-shaped vehicle probably means 175 to 200 miles, less in winter, and a small 400-volt pack won't fast-charge particularly quickly either. If you've read [my piece on range shopping](/posts/ev-range-shopping-madness), you know I think that's fine. Better than fine. You drive 40 miles a day. A 200-mile truck with a bed, a frunk, and more cabin room than a RAV4 for thirty grand is the vehicle I spent that whole article begging the industry to build. Ford built it. I don't get to flinch now.

## Slate should be nervous

The obvious comparison is Slate, the Bezos-backed startup with its $3,500-cheaper blank-slate pickup. The Out of Spec guys [spent a good chunk of their reaction video on this exact matchup](https://youtu.be/_WNP3S_PIEQ), and their conclusion was blunt: Slate's biggest problem is this truck. I respect what Slate is doing, but the math gets uncomfortable fast. The Slate has two doors, no screen, and famously no radio. The moment you option one up to what the Fathom includes standard, the price gap mostly evaporates, and Ford throws in a dealer on every corner and a service network that already exists. Slate buyers are enthusiasts making a statement. Fathom buyers will just be people who need a truck. There are a lot more of the second kind.

My one real gripe is driver assistance. Blue Cruise still only works in geofenced "hands-free blue zones," and it's a subscription on top. Meanwhile the rest of the market is moving to point-to-point systems that work on any road. For the price, I can't complain too loudly, but Ford knows this is where they're behind, and a customer-focused truck deserves better than a map of permission zones.

Pre-orders open in early 2027. I told you last August that Ford was betting the company on this team. The first card they turned over is a sub-$30,000 truck that actually hit its promised price. I'll be in line.
